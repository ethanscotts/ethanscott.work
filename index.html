Journey Through The Endless Castle

ONGOING | Progression · Fantasy · updates M,W,F

Sentenced to die after seeking revenge for his father's murder, Wulfric is given a second chance at life when his King discovers his hidden powers and commands him to climb The Endless Castle: A tower in the heart of an endless war. Compelled to complete this task before their rivals Wulfric strives to reunite with his lost brother. The fate of the world is at stake, will Wulfric and his men discover the source of the Castle's power? Or die trying?

Chapter 1 Sample

Wulfric was crouched and still, waiting in the rain. His feet slowly sank into the mud as he lingered. His dirty brown tunic was fully soaked through. He had been tracking his prey into the Eastern Marches for weeks and not once had it stopped raining. The winter wheat around him bent in the wind. The weather was worsening. Thunder rumbled in the distance. All I need is my axe, nothing more, he thought.

His thumb passed over the letters carved into the black wood handle of his axe. Harbardr, it read: his father's name.

Two of the murderers came out of the farm house; the house they had just stolen from some poor family that now hung, dead, in the orchard to the west. His eyes followed them as they slowly walked around the porch towards the east side of the building. They left a spear by the door as they went. One was of the same origin as himself, a man of the Sveiar. The other, it seemed, was a Moravian. They stopped and laughed as they urinated on the walls. Wretches. That was the only word he could conjure as he watched.

Years he had now spent, hunting these men down, hundreds of sleepless nights under the stars, many with an empty stomach. The filth who had killed his father and his uncle were finally before him. Wulfric felt sick at how pathetic they were. He waited for the moment he could isolate them one by one. Four of them, he believed there were, but only two had he seen so far.

Once the two of them had come back around the house, they finished their conversation and one of them sauntered back inside. The other stood by the stone steps in front, likely hewn and placed there by the former man of this house. This one's hair was long but well-kept. Wulfric could see him pull an ivory comb from a pocket inside his yellow wool mantle and begin to unravel his long black beard. The color of it reminded Wulfric of his brother Sigmund with his jet black hair. The man began to slowly walk towards the field.

Wulfric finally stood and his knees cracked. The overgrown winter grain still swayed over his head. The rainy breeze whipped his wet brown hair over his face. He loosened the grip on his father's wood-cutting axe. It slid further down where he now held it tightly at the heel. He rushed forth and buried the axe deep in the man's chest. The comb was cleaved in two and stuck in the now bloody beard. The door of the house burst open; Wulfric had made too much noise. Two more of those animals came running towards him. Quickly, he stood firm and chopped at the legs of the man on his left. The attacker tumbled to the ground, striking his head on the stone steps. He went limp instantly; the late farmer, it seemed, wanted his share of vengeance. The other brigand was quicker: he grabbed the spear that lay against the door frame and rushed at Wulfric, screaming.

The pain was sharp. The spear had found its place deep in his thigh. Dirty hands then grasped at his throat as they both fell into the mud. Wulfric's axe had flown to the wayside as they struggled on the ground, the spear rending his flesh as they moved. The agony was like lightning: it shot through his body in fast jolts that pounded in his ears. The beast on top of him freed one of his arms and twisted the spear. My axe is enough! he thought, yet the air around him shifted without his say. A thunderclap and flash. This time the lightning he felt was real. It came roaring from his fingertips, throwing the man back into the farm house with a trail of black smoke and red flame.

Managing to stand, Wulfric pulled the spear from his leg and, as fast as he could, climbed the stairs and stuck the bloodied end of the weapon into the wood, using the spear to wedge the door closed. He could already see the fire growing from under the door. Before he could back away, the thatched roof took light. The rain could not drown out the fire, nor could it drown out the screams. Father, if you're watching, see your works in me. He looked down at his hands where soot had stained the tips of each finger. Just as he had the mind to run, soldiers from the nearby village came upon the farm house. There's no point in it now, it seems.
